The fine print, in plain English

What NYC actually requires about labeling bins

Short version: the city does not make most homes label their trash bin - but two kinds of bins must be labeled to be collected, and bigger buildings have their own labeling duties. Here's the line between rule and rumor.

Checked against NYC & DSNY sources · Last checked November 5, 2026

The rules

Trash: the bin is mandated, the label isn't (for small buildings)

Buildings with 1 to 9 residential units must set out trash in the official NYC Bin - that's been the rule since June 1, 2026, with a $50 fine in effect since September 8, 2026. The city does not require those homes to write an address on the bin. It requires the bin itself.

Recycling and compost: the label is the rule

No official bin is required for recycling or compost - but whatever bin you use must be labeled for what's in it. Metal, glass, plastic and paper go in any labeled bin with a lid, 55 gallons or less (or in clear bags). Compost goes in any sealed, clearly labeled bin of 55 gallons or less. An unlabeled recycling bin risks being skipped at the curb.

Bigger buildings: labeling and signs are part of the job

Buildings with 4 or more units have to keep their bins and waste storage areas clearly labeled and signed, and can be fined when they aren't. That's the building's responsibility, not the tenants'.

The myths

"The city makes you buy a label from them"

There is no city-issued label and nothing to buy from the city. Every official NYC Bin comes with a space for your address, and how you mark it is up to you - a marker works, a printed label works better.

"Writing on the bin is defacing city property"

The bin is yours - you bought it. The city includes the address space so owners can mark their bins for easy identification. Labeling yours is using the bin as intended.

"If your bin has no address, the city can fine you"

For 1 to 9 unit homes, the fines are about the bin and the setout - wrong container, loose trash, trash out too early - not about a missing address. Labeling is still the cheapest insurance against mix-ups and walk-offs, which is why this site exists. But it isn't a ticket item.

Why label anyway?

Mix-ups

A block of identical bins is a nightly shell game. An address ends it.

Walk-offs

If a bin is stolen, DSNY does not replace it - the remedy is a police report and a new bin at your expense. A bin with a big address on it is harder to take quietly.

Proof

Pair the label with a photo of your bin's serial number and you can prove which bin is yours. NumberCan covers that.
